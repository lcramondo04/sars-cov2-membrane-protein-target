# SARS-CoV-2 Membrane Protein 7Y9B: Drug Target Investigation

**Larissa Nogueira Gomes, Jenna Desiderio, Lucas Ramondo**
Worcester Polytechnic Institute

📄 [Full Infographic (PDF)](./infographic/SARS-Covid_Infographic.pdf)

## Overview

As of May 2023, COVID-19 had caused roughly 6 million deaths worldwide. This project investigated the SARS-CoV-2 membrane (M) protein — the most abundant structural protein in the virus, central to viral assembly — as a potential drug target, with a focus on identifying conserved regions that could be exploited therapeutically.

## Approach

- Used UniProt and BLAST-based nucleotide searches to investigate conserved regions across coronavirus membrane proteins
- Identified and located the target structure **PDB 7Y9B** in the Protein Data Bank
- Compared amino acid sequences across multiple human coronavirus strains (OC43, HKU1, 229E) to identify low E-value, highly conserved motifs
- Pinpointed a conserved segment (YFV-S–L–R-TSMWSFNPE, codons 101–121) that partially overlaps the interior membrane boundary of the protein's third transmembrane domain (TMIII) — conserved at 70–100% consensus across coronaviruses
- Proposed an allosteric modulator strategy: an orthosteric antagonist targeting the TMIII domain to block interaction between the M protein and the nucleocapsid (N) protein, potentially disrupting viral assembly

## Key Findings

- The identified motif is the most conserved region of the M protein across coronavirus species, suggesting it plays a functionally important, potentially interaction-critical role (hypothesized to relate to N protein and viral RNA binding)
- The transmembrane domain — a common site for ligand binding and conformational gating in membrane proteins — was proposed as a viable target for an allosteric drug candidate designed to block M–N protein interaction

## Tools & Techniques

UniProt, BLAST (nucleotide), RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB), sequence conservation/consensus analysis
